McLaren  |  F1
img — three-quarter front
The Central Driving Position

Gordon Murray placed the driver in the centre of the F1, flanked by two passenger seats set slightly back. The result is a cockpit that feels like a single-seat racer made road-legal — total symmetry, total focus, and a sightline no conventional supercar can match.

img — gold-lined engine bay
A Bay Lined in Gold

The 6.1-litre BMW V12 sits beneath an engine bay lined with gold foil — chosen purely for its thermal reflectivity. It is the kind of no-compromise engineering decision that defines the car: function first, and the spectacle follows.

img — carbon monocoque
Carbon, Before It Was Common

The F1 was the first production road car built around a full carbon-fibre monocoque. At a time when the material was reserved for Formula 1, it set a structural template the entire hypercar industry would spend the next thirty years catching up to.

img — 1998 Le Mans livery
240 mph, and a Le Mans Win

A car never intended to race won Le Mans outright in 1995, and the road car held the production-car speed record for over a decade at 240.1 mph. Few machines have ever been so complete straight out of the box.
